## 2. Know your two assets

- **`LinkedIn_Post.md`** — the short native post. Highest reach. Use this as your main launch post.
- **`LinkedIn_Article.md`** — the long-form article. Publish as a LinkedIn Article (Newsletter) for depth/SEO and link to it from the post.

Rule of thumb: **post drives reach, article drives depth.** Lead with the post; link the article for people who want the full argument.

---

## 3. Publish — step by step

1. **Publish the Article first** (LinkedIn → Write article). Paste `LinkedIn_Article.md`. Add a clear cover image and the title "The difference was never the model — it's the method."
2. **Wait ~1–2 hours**, then publish the native **Post** (`LinkedIn_Post.md`). LinkedIn favors native text posts, so paste the text directly — don't lead with an external link (put the link in the first comment or after "Comment KIT").
3. **First comment:** drop the article link + a one-liner ("Full write-up + the free General prompt here 👇").
4. **CTA flow:** when people comment "KIT" or DM, reply with the **checkout link** (never the file). If you want it hands-off, add the link to the post itself after the CTA line.
5. **Reply to every comment in the first 2 hours** — early engagement drives the algorithm.

---

## 4. Formatting rules for LinkedIn (so it performs)

- Strong first line (the "hook") — it's the only thing shown before "see more." Yours is "We keep blaming the model." Keep it.
- Short lines, whitespace between them. No walls of text.
- 3–5 relevant hashtags at the end (already in the post).
- One idea per line for the arrows (→). Emojis sparingly.
- Don't edit the post for ~30 min after publishing (edits can suppress reach on some accounts).

## 6. Content engine — prompts to generate MORE posts

Use the General prompt as system prompt, then one of these:

**Post generator:**
> Write a LinkedIn post (120–200 words) about [topic]. Hook in the first line. Short lines, whitespace. One concrete story or number. End with a soft CTA to comment a keyword. 3–5 hashtags. No hype, no clichés, no "I'm thrilled to announce." Match this voice: honest, technical, slightly contrarian.

**Hook variations:**
> Give me 10 first-line hooks for a LinkedIn post about [topic]. Each under 12 words, curiosity or contrarian angle, no clickbait I can't back up.

**Repurpose the article:**
> From this article [paste], extract 5 standalone LinkedIn posts, each built around one idea with its own hook and CTA.

**Comment-to-DM reply:**
> Write 3 short, warm replies I can send when someone comments "KIT" — each includes the checkout link and one line of value, no pushiness.

## 7. Metrics to watch

- Post: impressions, comment count (KIT comments = intent), profile visits.
- Funnel: free-prompt email signups → paid conversions → 1:1 bookings.
- If comments are high but sales low: the checkout friction or price framing is off, not the post.

---

## 8. Honest guardrails

- Don't fake engagement or buy comments — this audience detects it and it kills trust.
- Keep the "not foolproof, gaps found + fixed, limits disclosed" framing everywhere. It's your differentiator, not a weakness.
- Deliver the buyer kit only (no marketing files). Rotate any secret/API key before sharing anything derived from your real projects.
